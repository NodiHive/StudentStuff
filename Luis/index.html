Falcons

The fastest animals on Earth — built for speed, precision, and the wide open sky.

Falcons are raptors built around one idea: speed. With long, tapered wings and a streamlined body, they hunt on the wing and strike with astonishing precision. The peregrine falcon shown here is the headline act — a bird found on every continent except Antarctica, and the fastest living creature we know of.

Interesting facts

1 They're the fastest animal alive
In a hunting dive — called a stoop — a peregrine falcon can exceed 240 miles per hour (about 390 km/h). Nothing else on the planet moves faster. Special bony baffles in their nostrils let them breathe even at those speeds, and a translucent third eyelid keeps their eyes clear and protected during the dive.
2 Their eyesight is roughly 8× sharper than ours
A falcon can spot a pigeon-sized target from over a mile away. Their eyes are so large relative to their skulls that if humans had the same ratio, our eyes would be the size of oranges. That dark "teardrop" marking below the eye — visible in these photos — is thought to cut glare from the sun, like the black stripes athletes wear.
3 They thrive in cities
Skyscrapers make excellent artificial cliffs. Peregrines now nest on buildings and bridges in cities worldwide, hunting the abundant pigeons below. After the pesticide DDT nearly wiped them out in the mid-1900s, urban nesting sites helped drive one of conservation's great comeback stories.
4 The female is the bigger, bolder hunter
Female falcons are noticeably larger than males — up to about a third bigger. In falconry the male is even called a "tiercel," from the Latin for "a third." The larger female can take down bigger prey, while the smaller, nimbler male is often the faster flier.
5 Those talons do the work
A falcon often doesn't grab prey in the dive — it strikes with a clenched foot at high speed, stunning or killing the target on impact, then circles back to catch it. The hooked "tooth" on their beak, called the tomial tooth, is used to sever the spine of their catch quickly.

The family tree
